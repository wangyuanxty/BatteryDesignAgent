We are developing a grid-storage cell on the Chen2020 NMC811/graphite profile. The cathode is the free variable: everything else is fixed, including the electrolyte (EC/EMC with LiPF6) and the cell architecture. We need a cathode composition that gives us a *cell we can actually operate*, not a number: the design has to be compatible with the components we have, and it has to pass the cell-level criteria below.

Two things define the target.

(1) A high-voltage cathode. We want a cathode whose computed average voltage reaches 4.6 V or more through our CHGNet screening machinery. That is above the band of common layered and olivine cathodes and inside the regime where real 4.6-4.8 V cathodes operate (LiNi0.5Mn1.5O4-type spinels, LiCoPO4-class olivines), so this part of the window is chosen to be reachable by chemistry that exists.

(2) Compatibility with the fixed cell. The electrolyte we have is not a high-voltage electrolyte: carbonate solvents with LiPF6 are anodically limited in the 4.8 V range. A cathode that charges above that limit cannot be operated in this cell, whatever its computed voltage - so the design must also satisfy: charging potential <= 4.8 V (the declared anodic limit of the fixed electrolyte). A candidate that reaches 4.6 V but requires charging above 4.8 V is not a design we can use.

The composition must come from outside our documented catalogue. We have compiled 115 documented cathode materials and computed 102 screening points (average voltage, capacity, relative stability) with the same machinery; the candidate must not be one of those materials and its computed point must lie outside the convex hull of those 102 points.   (and the --point path for the computed point).

What we can compute, and what we cannot. Our machinery builds six structure families: layered LiMO2, olivine LiMPO4, spinel LiM2O4, tavorite LiM(PO4)F, tavorite LiM(SO4)F, and NASICON Li3M2(PO4)3. Compositions in any other family cannot be screened - the tool reports them as unsupported rather than approximating them; treat that as a hard boundary of this task.

Calibration, measured against literature before this run: olivine LiFePO4 computes 3.443 V vs 3.4 V (control); tavorite sulfate LiFeSO4F 3.436 vs 3.6; tavorite phosphate LiVPO4F 3.580 vs ~4.2; NASICON Li3V2(PO4)3 3.347 vs 3.8; spinel LiMn2O4 3.77 vs ~4.0 and LiNi0.5Mn1.5O4 4.334 vs ~4.7. V-bearing and spinel families compute systematically low by roughly 0.2-0.5 V - plan for that margin. Ni-rich olivines compute inflated voltages (two such materials are excluded from the hull for that reason, and the former band top, 5.114 V, is one of them); Fe substitution in the tavorite-phosphate and NASICON families is not validated (LiFePO4F computes 4.919 V against a literature 2.75 V, Li3Fe2(PO4)3 4.699 against 2.8).
